You’ve picked a date to switch operations. Now put yourself in the dispatcher’s seat. The first work order comes in after the switch. Where does it go? What if the customer is calling about a job a crew already started?

On a [paving bid and work-order transition](/work/paving-bid-and-transition) I helped coordinate, crews still had jobs open while the company was deciding how to route new work. Both kinds of work would be moving at the same time.

## Write the rule dispatch can use

The plan was simple enough to state in two sentences. Open jobs stay with the operation that started them. New work orders go to the new operation once the switch takes effect.

Then walk through what those sentences mean at the desk. If an existing job needs another visit, where does the update go? If a new order comes in with incomplete information, who decides where to send it? Who checks that the crew received it? Get those answers from the people doing the work and write them down where they’ll look when the phone rings.

## Test one order on each side of the switch

Before the change, take an open job and a sample new order. Ask dispatch to show you which queue each one goes into, who owns it, and where the next person can see its status. If two people give you different answers, you’ve found a decision to settle before go-live.

On the first day, check the first few real orders. If one lands in the wrong place, correct its routing and find out whether the intake instructions, the system, or the ownership rule needs to change. Give that fix an owner.
