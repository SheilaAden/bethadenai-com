Ask three people how they do the same recurring calculation. You might get a spreadsheet, a phone calculator, and the name of the person everyone asks when the numbers look off.

Each of those answers tells you something about how the work really gets done. The spreadsheet shows what gets entered. The person people ask knows which inputs to trust and when the result deserves a second look. A workflow has to capture both.

## Put the decisions where the calculation happens

Proofmaster™ is a calculator built around a distillery workflow. What makes this kind of tool valuable is the routine it gives the team. Everyone should be able to see what to enter, which units to use, what the output means, and when to stop and check a result.

Start by having the person who knows the process walk you through a real example. Watch where they pause, which values they double-check, and what they do when information is missing. Those moments are where the instructions need to be clearest.

## Test it with the next person who’ll use it

Try an example the team knows well. Confirm that the result agrees with the trusted reference and that a second person can work through it without coaching. Then try an incomplete input or a unit mix-up. The tool should make it clear what needs correcting before anyone acts on the result.

Once the inputs, units, checks, and next action are consistent, you’ve documented a piece of work someone else can pick up. The calculator is where that workflow gets used.
